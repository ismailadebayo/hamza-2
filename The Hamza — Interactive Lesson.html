The Hamza

الهَمْزَة (أ / ا)

الهَمْزَةُ (أ / ا)
Two Hamzas That Look Almost Alike

Arabic has two kinds of hamza. One is written plainly and disappears in speech. The other always carries the hamza mark and is always heard. Mixing them up changes how a word is read aloud. Tap through to tell them apart.

همزة الوصل — silent همزة القطع — always heard
The big picture

هَمْزَةُ الوَصْل وَهَمْزَةُ القَطْعTwo hamzas, two behaviors

ا
Hamzat al-Waṣl
“Connecting hamza.” Written as a plain alif, no hamza mark. Only pronounced at the very start of speech — it goes silent whenever the word before it ends in a vowel.
أ / إ
Hamzat al-Qaṭ‘
“Cutting hamza.” Always written with the hamza mark — أ for fatḥa/ḍamma, إ for kasra. Always pronounced, no matter what comes before it.

Hear the difference:

مِنِ اسْمٍ → pronounced مِنِسْمٍ
the alif of اسم vanishes into the kasra before it — hamzat al-waṣl
مِنْ أَخٍ → pronounced مِنْ أَخٍ
the hamza of أخ stays fully voiced — hamzat al-qaṭ‘

Why it matters

Watch it disappear in recitation

This isn't just theory — it changes how a real ayah is read aloud.

سَبِّحِ اسْمَ رَبِّكَ الأَعْلَى
“Glorify the name of your Lord, the Most High” — Sūrah al-A‘lā, 87:1

Written separately, the alif of اسْمَ is there. But joined in speech, it vanishes into the kasra before it:

سَبِّحِ+ اسْمَ= سَبِّحِسْمَ
i
The definite article الأعلى keeps its own hamzat al-waṣl too, but it's swallowed the same way after رَبِّكَ.
Hamzat al-Qaṭ‘ · The Basics

هَمْزَةُ القَطْعAlways written, always heard

The rule Every noun, verb, and particle that starts with a hamza carries hamzat al-qaṭ‘ unless it's on the short list you just saw. Write أ for fatḥa/ḍamma, إ for kasra — and pronounce it every single time.
أَخٌ
a brother
أُمٌّ
a mother
إِجْلَالٌ
glorification
Hamzat al-Qaṭ‘ · In Context

Even a tiny prefix doesn't silence it

Attach لَـ, وَـ, سَـ, or الـ (as a true qaṭ‘ noun) in front — the hamza is still fully voiced.

الأُمُّ
the mother
لَأُكْرِمَنَّ
I will surely honor
لِأَنَّكَ
because you
لَزَيْدٌ
surely, Zayd
سَأَفْعَلُ
I will do
وَأُمٌّ
and a mother
i
Compare this to سَأَفْعَلُ — the سَـ is just a prefix meaning “will”; the hamza belongs to فَعَلَ itself, so it's qaṭ‘ and stays fully pronounced.
One more detail

In the middle of a word, hamza needs a “seat”
